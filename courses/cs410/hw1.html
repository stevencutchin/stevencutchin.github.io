Pick one notation and stay consistent within a diagram.

Part 1  ·  Conceptual Model 40 points

An automobile repair shop needs a database to manage its sales of parts and services (for example, oil changes, replacing brake pads, a tire rotation). Here are some of the things they need to track:

  • Customers are billed and pay in terms of an invoice, describing the parts (or items) sold and the service done for a particular transaction.
  • Each invoice is sold by a particular employee — the one who rang up the final transaction.
  • On a given invoice, customers can buy various items (parts, accessories, and so on). Service performed also appears as line items on the invoice.
  • Items sold have a description, a quantity, and a unit price. They also have an item ID / SKU to tie them into an inventory system.
    • A service is performed by a particular employee, and has a description and a cost.
    • Tip: A service can also be modeled with a quantity and a unit price — an hourly billing rate for general labor, and a quantity of 1 for a service with a standardized billing rate (such as $40 for a tire rotation).
  • Service is also performed on a specific vehicle.
  • Customers can have more than one vehicle.
  • Invoices can be open — still accumulating items — or closed, meaning they are finished and ready to be paid.
  • Track basic information about customers (name, billing address, phone number, and so on).

Simplifying assumptions

  • Each invoice is sold to a single customer.
  • Each vehicle is owned by a single customer.
  • You do not need to model an inventory or a payroll system.
Deliverable

Using ER syntax, draw a single integrated conceptual model for the mini-world described above.

Part 2  ·  Relational Model 20 points

Describe a set of relations that will store the data from your ER model in Part 1. For each relation, give:

  • Its name.
  • The entity or relationship type it stores.
  • Its attributes, with each one's approximate data type (number, string, date).
  • Its primary key.
  • Its foreign key(s), and the relations they reference.
Deliverable

A complete list of relations with the five items above spelled out for each.

Part 3  ·  Example Data 10 points

For each of your relations in Part 2, give 2–5 example rows.

All data must be referentially valid: every foreign-key value in one table must refer to a valid primary-key value in the table it references.

Deliverable

A small, consistent set of sample rows for every relation, with foreign keys that point at real rows.

Part 4  ·  Enhanced Entity–Relationship (EER) Model 20 points

Consider the following scenario:

An international airline offers flights, each of which has a number that identifies the flight (for example, Paris–Boston), a date (25 March 2019), a departure time (8:00 AM) and an arrival time (12:00 PM), a departure airport and an arrival airport. There are national and international flights. International flights can have one or more stopovers.

4.1   Conceptual model — 10 points

Draw a conceptual model for the scenario above, using the generalization construct of the Enhanced Entity–Relationship model where appropriate. In each case, indicate the attributes of the various entities and the type of generalization, resolving the cases of overlapping.

4.2   Relational translation — 10 points

Provide a translation into the relational model of the conceptual model you designed in 4.1.
